Diffusion Across a Membrane

A box with a wall down the middle and a few holes in it. Each walker moves one fixed step in a uniformly random direction, over and over. That is the whole rule: none of them can tell which side is crowded, and none of them is pushed anywhere. Watch the two sides even out anyway — and watch the traffic, which is heavy in both directions at once. The flow is only the difference between them.

Formulas
  • The rule: one step of length L, random direction, every h seconds
  • Spreading: ⟨r²⟩ = 4Dt, with D = L²/4h
  • Fick's first law: J = P·ΔN
  • Evening out: ΔN(t) = ΔN₀·e^(−t/τ)
Notes
  • Nothing here knows where the crowd is. Each walker picks a direction at random with no reference to anything, and the two sides even out because there are simply more walkers on the busy side to wander across. There is no force. There is only counting.
  • Watch the two crossing rates, not just the net. At full imbalance about a quarter more walkers go out than come back — the rest of the traffic cancels. When the sides finally match, both rates stay high and the net falls to nothing. Equilibrium is not stillness.
  • A bigger hole passes more, but not in proportion: eight times the opening gives about three times the flow. In two dimensions a walker that misses the hole slides along the wall and tries again, so what limits the traffic is finding the hole rather than fitting through it.
  • The colours say where each walker started, not which side it is on now. Start them all on the left and the right-hand box fills with left-coloured dots — that is the mixing, and it never sorts itself back out.
