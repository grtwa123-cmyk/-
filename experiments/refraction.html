Refraction & Total Internal Reflection

A ray of light crosses from one medium into another and bends by Snell's law. Drag the incident ray or use the sliders; when it travels into a less dense medium beyond the critical angle, it stops crossing and reflects entirely.

Formulas
  • Snell's law: n₁·sin θ₁ = n₂·sin θ₂
  • Critical angle (n₁ > n₂): θ_c = arcsin(n₂/n₁)
  • Phase speed: v = c / n
  • Reflectance (unpolarised): R = (R_s + R_p) / 2
Notes
  • Into a denser medium (n₂ > n₁) the ray bends toward the normal; into a rarer medium it bends away.
  • Total internal reflection only happens going from dense to rare (n₁ > n₂), once θ₁ exceeds the critical angle — this is how optical fibres trap light.
  • The reflected and refracted brightness follow the Fresnel equations: reflectance rises toward 100% as θ₁ approaches grazing incidence or the critical angle.
