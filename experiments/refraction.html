Refraction & Total Internal Reflection

Nothing here applies Snell's law. Each point of the interface, as the incoming wavefront sweeps across it, starts a Huygens wavelet spreading into the second medium at c/n₂ — and the refracted wavefront is the one straight line those wavelets are all tangent to. The page searches for that line; θ₂ is what the search returns, and n₁·sin θ₁ = n₂·sin θ₂ comes out as a measurement. Past the critical angle no such line exists, which is all total internal reflection is.

Formulas
  • Put in — phase speed: v = c / n
  • Put in — the sweep: t(x) = x·sin θ₁ / v₁
  • Put in — each wavelet: r(x) = v₂·(T − t(x))
  • Searched for — the tangent: min over θ of spread(x·sin θ + r(x))
  • Comes out — Snell's law: n₁·sin θ₁ = n₂·sin θ₂
  • Formula — reflectance (unpolarised): R = (R_s + R_p) / 2
Notes
  • The construction is told only how fast light goes in each medium and when the wavefront reaches each point of the interface. It is never told Snell's law — arcsin((n₁/n₂)·sin θ₁) appears nowhere in the code. The two readouts either side of the equals sign are obtained independently, and they agree to the last digit shown.
  • Total internal reflection is not a special case bolted on. Past the critical angle the wavelets outrun the sweep, no single line can touch them all, and the search closes on nothing — turn the wavelets on and watch the tangent fail to exist. The critical angle in the readout is found by bisecting on exactly that failure, not by arcsin(n₂/n₁).
  • Brightness is the one thing still taken from a formula: the Fresnel equations for unpolarised light come from matching fields at the boundary, not from this geometry. They are evaluated at the measured θ₂.
  • Fermat's principle is the same law by another route — the path of least time bends by exactly this much. The checks drive both and confirm they pick out the same geometry to one part in 10⁷.
