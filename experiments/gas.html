Ideal Gas & Kinetic Theory

Gas particles rattle around a chamber sealed by a piston. The pressure gauge is honest — it sums real momentum kicks on the walls — and sits right on the ideal-gas isotherm. Drag the piston (or the sliders) and watch P, V, T negotiate.

Formulas
  • Equation of state (2D): P·A = N·k·T
  • RMS speed: vᵣₘₛ = √(2kT/m)
  • Pressure from impacts: P = Σ 2m|v⊥| / (Δt · L)
Notes
  • Pressure is nothing but wall collisions: the measured value (impulse per time per wall length) fluctuates around NkT/V, and the fluctuations shrink as N grows.
  • At constant temperature P ∝ 1/V (Boyle's law). Push the piston in and the dot climbs the hyperbolic isotherm.
  • An ideal gas has no interactions — particles never collide with each other here, only with the walls. A thermostat rescales speeds so T stays where you set it (isothermal compression).
