Reaction Rates & Collision Theory

A (red) and B (blue) particles collide constantly, but colliding isn't enough — a reaction needs the collision energy to beat the activation barrier Ea. Heat the mixture or lower the barrier and watch the purple product appear faster, with the measured success rate landing on the Boltzmann factor e^(−Ea/kT).

Formulas
  • Reaction: A + B → C
  • Success per collision: P = e^(−Ea/kT)
  • Arrhenius: k = A·e^(−Ea/RT)
  • Condition (line of centres): E⟂ = ½μ(v_rel·n̂)² ≥ Ea
Notes
  • Most collisions fail. Only the rare, energetic ones — the exponential tail of the Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution — clear the activation barrier.
  • Temperature is exponential leverage: e^(−Ea/kT) means a modest warming can multiply the reaction rate many times over — the essence of Arrhenius behaviour.
  • The energy criterion is measured along the line of centres at contact; for hard disks that makes the energetic-collision fraction exactly e^(−Ea/kT) — watch the two bars meet as collisions accumulate. Of those energetic collisions, only a fraction (the steric factor behind Arrhenius' A) actually react, so the mixture burns at a realistic pace.
