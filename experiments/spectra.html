Hydrogen Spectrum

Lift the electron to a higher shell and let go. It falls back down one jump at a time, and every jump throws out a photon whose colour is fixed by the energy it lost. Collect enough of them and the bright lines of the Balmer series appear — the same four colours you see through a real spectroscope.

Formulas
  • Energy level: Eₙ = −13.6 eV / n²
  • Photon: ΔE = Eₙ₂ − Eₙ₁ = hc / λ
  • Rydberg: 1/λ = Rₕ(1/n₁² − 1/n₂²)
  • Series: n₁ = 1 Lyman, 2 Balmer, 3 Paschen
Notes
  • The electron can only sit on the rungs, never between them, so only certain energies can ever be released — that is why the spectrum is sharp lines instead of a smooth rainbow.
  • Only the Balmer series (landing on n = 2) falls in visible light. Lyman jumps land on n = 1 and are far into the ultraviolet; Paschen jumps land on n = 3, out in the infrared.
  • The rungs crowd together as n grows because the energy goes as 1/n². Above the n = ∞ limit the electron is free and the atom is ionised, which takes 13.6 eV from the ground state.
