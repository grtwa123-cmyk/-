Pendulums

Three pendulums that each show something different. A wave pendulum turns a row of slightly different periods into a travelling pattern; Foucault's pendulum keeps swinging in one plane while the ground turns under it; Newton's cradle passes a collision straight through a row of touching balls.

Formulas
  • Period (small angle): T = 2π · √(L / g)
  • Equation of motion: θ̈ = −(g/L)·sin(θ) − b·θ̇
  • Foucault (rotating frame): ζ̈ + 2iΩₒζ̇ + ω²ζ = 0
  • Precession: Tₚ = Tₑₖₔₖₕₖₑₒ / |sin φ|
  • Equal-mass elastic collision: v₁′ = v₂, v₂′ = v₁
What to look for
  • Each pendulum is a little shorter than the last, so each runs at a slightly higher frequency. Nothing is choreographed — the travelling wave, the split into two counter-rotating strands, and the moment they all line up again are just those frequencies drifting in and out of step.
  • The period depends on length and gravity but not on mass, and only weakly on amplitude. Push θ₀ past about 30° and the real period creeps above the small-angle 2π√(L/g).
What to look for
  • The pendulum keeps swinging in one plane; it is the ground that turns. Seen from above, the plane creeps clockwise in the northern hemisphere and counter-clockwise in the southern, knocking over the ring of pegs one by one.
  • Only the vertical part of Earth's rotation counts, so the plane turns at Ω·sin φ and a full turn takes one sidereal day divided by |sin φ| — 23.93 h at the pole, 31.8 h in Paris, and never at the equator. Slide the latitude to zero and the precession stops dead.
  • Real time runs far too slowly to watch, so the clock is accelerated; the readouts show simulated time, not yours. At high acceleration the swing itself blurs into a bar — that bar is the plane.
What to look for
  • Pull back one ball and one ball leaves; pull back three and exactly three leave. Nothing here counts balls — the collision rule is only that two equal masses hitting head-on elastically swap velocities, and the count comes out of it.
  • That result needs the collisions to happen one after another, which is why the balls rest a hair apart rather than in perfect contact — real cradles do too. A genuinely simultaneous multi-ball collision has no unique answer.
  • Momentum and kinetic energy are both conserved at every strike, which is what forces k in to give k out: any other number would satisfy one law and break the other. The energy drift readout is the integrator checking itself.
