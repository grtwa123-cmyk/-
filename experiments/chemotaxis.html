Chemotaxis — Run and Tumble

A bacterium is a few thousandths of a millimetre long. At that size it is shaken so hard by the water around it that it cannot hold a heading, and it certainly cannot tell whether its front end is in more food than its back end. It has no compass. All it can do is swim in a straight line for a while, stop, pick a new direction at random, and go again — and remember what the concentration was a moment ago. When things are getting better it waits a little longer before tumbling. That is the whole of it, and it is enough to climb a hill.

Formulas
  • The rule: rate = (1/τ)·max(0, 1 − β·dc/dt)
  • Tumble rate by heading: λ(θ) = λ₀(1 − k cos θ), k = βv/W
  • Spreading: ⟨r²⟩ = 2v²τ²(t/τ − 1 + e^(−t/τ))
  • Its two limits: v²t² early, 4Dt late, D = v²τ/2
  • Drift: v_d = (v/k)(1 − √(1−k²)) ≈ vk/2
  • Pile-up: ρ(x) ∝ e^(x/ℓ), ℓ = D/v_d = τW/β
Notes
  • Nothing in the code asks which way the attractant lies. Each cell subtracts the concentration it felt one sub-step ago from the one it feels now, and lets that number lengthen or shorten its run. Sort the sub-steps afterwards by the angle between heading and gradient and a clean straight line appears — a direction the cell never measured, recovered from a cell that only compared two numbers in time.
  • Turn the memory off and watch the lower graph. On the left the population spreads along the steep line: over less than one run the cells are simply moving, and the distance grows with the time, so its square grows with the square of the time. On the right that bends into the shallower line of a random walk. The single curve through both is the only honest description, and the diffusive limit alone is twenty times wrong at a tenth of a run.
  • The pile-up stops growing. Drift carries cells up the gradient, spreading carries them back down, and where the two balance the population is an exponential with a length of ℓ = D/v_d. That is not a wall the cells are pressed against — the traffic through every line in the dish is heavy, and only the difference has gone to zero.
  • Push the swimming speed from 40 to 120 and the cluster does not tighten. A cell that swims three times as fast drifts nine times as fast, because it also perceives the gradient three times as steeply — and it spreads nine times as fast too. ℓ = τW/β has no v in it at all.
  • The dish has walls, and a walk that keeps bouncing off them stops getting further away, which would make the spreading measurement read low by four fifths. A reflection is a mirror, so each cell also carries the path it would have taken had the wall not been there. That unfolded path is what the graph plots — exact, as long as the tumble rate is blind to direction, which is why the curve is only claimed with the memory set to zero.
