Nerve Impulse — Hodgkin–Huxley

Four coupled differential equations, integrated exactly as Hodgkin and Huxley wrote them in 1952. Two kinds of gate open and close, each at its own speed, and that is the whole model. No line of code asks whether the voltage has crossed a threshold — yet a threshold is there, and it is sharp: a hair below it the membrane just sags back, a hair above it and the axon fires a full spike of the same size every time.

Formulas
  • Membrane: Cₘ dV/dt = I − g̅ₘₐm³h(V−Eₘₐ) − g̅ₖn⁴(V−Eₖ) − gₗ(V−Eₗ)
  • Gates: dx/dt = αₓ(V)(1−x) − βₓ(V)x, x = m, h, n
  • Constants: g̅ₘₐ=120, g̅ₖ=36, gₗ=0.3 mS/cm²
  • Reversal: Eₘₐ=+50, Eₖ=−77, Eₗ=−54.4 mV
What to look for
  • Nothing in the model tests the voltage against a threshold. The four equations are integrated as written and the spike is what they do. Press Find threshold and the search will bracket it to three decimals: one part in a thousand below, the membrane sags back and nothing happens; one part above, a full action potential. That cliff is the whole point — it is a property of the gate kinetics, not a rule that was imposed.
  • Once over the line, the spike is the same size however hard you push. Its height comes from Eₘₐ, not from the stimulus, which is why a nerve carries information as a rate of firing rather than an amplitude.
  • Watch the gates rather than the voltage. m opens in a fraction of a millisecond and drags V up towards Eₘₐ; h closes more slowly and shuts the sodium off; n opens later still and pulls V down past rest. The refractory period is just h needing time to reopen — try the pulse pair and shorten the gap until the second spike disappears.
  • Sustained current does something odd twice over. Firing does not ease in from zero — below about 6 µA/cm² there is nothing, and above it the axon starts straight in at roughly 50 Hz. Then push past about 80 and firing stops: so much current holds V high enough that h never recovers. Too much stimulus is as silent as too little.
