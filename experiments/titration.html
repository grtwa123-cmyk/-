Formulas
  • Charge balance: [H⁺] + [Na⁺] = [OH⁻] + [A⁻]
  • Buffer (Henderson–Hasselbalch): pH = pKₐ + log([A⁻]/[HA])
  • Equivalence volume: Vₑq = Cₐ·Vₐ / Cₒ
  • Ion product of water: [H⁺][OH⁻] = 10⁻¹⁴
Notes
  • A strong acid–strong base curve hugs low pH, then leaps ~6 pH units within a fraction of a millilitre around the equivalence point, passing pH 7 exactly at equivalence.
  • A weak acid resists in the buffer region: at half-equivalence pH = pKₐ (4.74 for acetic acid). Its equivalence point sits above 7 because the conjugate base hydrolyses.
  • Phenolphthalein turns pink between pH 8.2 and 10 — inside the vertical jump for both acids here, which is exactly what makes it a good indicator for these titrations.
