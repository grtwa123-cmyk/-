Lenses & Image Formation

Put an object in front of a lens and an image appears somewhere — real or virtual, upright or inverted, bigger or smaller. Nothing here is drawn to the answer: a whole fan of rays leaves the object, each one is bent by the same thin-lens rule, and where they cross is the image. Slide the object toward the focal point and watch them stop converging altogether.

Formulas
  • Thin lens: 1/v − 1/u = 1/f
  • Magnification: m = v/u
  • Refraction of one ray: θ′ = θ − y/f
  • Newton's form: x·x′ = f²
  • Lensmaker: 1/f = (n−1)(1/R₁ − 1/R₂)
What to look for
  • Every ray in the fan is bent by the same one-line rule — a ray crossing the lens at height y has its slope changed by −y/f, and that is the whole of the lens. The image is simply where they all end up crossing. Turn the ray count up and they keep landing on the same point.
  • The three rays from the textbook are not special rules, just three members of that fan whose paths are easy to draw: the one arriving parallel leaves through the far focus, the one through the centre carries straight on, and the one through the near focus leaves parallel.
  • Walk the object in from far away with a converging lens. The image starts small and inverted just past the focus, grows as the object nears 2f, is exactly life-size at 2f, and runs away to infinity as the object reaches f. Cross inside f and the rays stop converging — the image flips to virtual, upright and magnified. That is a magnifying glass.
  • A diverging lens never converges anything: for any real object the rays spread, and only their backward extensions meet. The image is always virtual, upright and smaller, which is why the readout stays on the same side as the object.
