Electrolysis of Water

Push current through water and it splits: hydrogen bubbles off the cathode, oxygen off the anode, in exactly a 2:1 volume ratio. Below the decomposition voltage of 1.23 V nothing happens at all — raise the voltage and watch Faraday's laws fill the tubes.

Controls

2.50 V
Formulas
  • Overall: 2 H₂O → 2 H₂ + O₂
  • Cathode (−): 2 H₂O + 2 e⁻ → H₂ + 2 OH⁻
  • Anode (+): 2 H₂O → O₂ + 4 H⁺ + 4 e⁻
  • Faraday: n(H₂) = Q/2F, n(O₂) = Q/4F
Notes
  • Water only decomposes above E° = 1.23 V — below that the cell passes no current, no matter how long you wait. (Real cells need extra overpotential on top.)
  • The 2:1 volume ratio is pure stoichiometry: every four electrons make two H₂ at the cathode but only one O₂ at the anode. The gas columns here are computed from Q = ∫I dt, not animated by hand.
  • Pure water is a poor conductor — real demonstrations dissolve a little electrolyte (e.g. Na₂SO₄) so ions can carry the current between the electrodes.
