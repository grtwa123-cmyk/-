Double-Slit Interference & Diffraction

Nothing here evaluates a diffraction formula. Every point of every open slit is treated as a source, all in phase, and the amplitude in a direction is the sum of them all — which separates, because the slits are alike, into one slit interfering with itself times the slits interfering with each other. The intensity is then scanned down the screen and the fringes are located as peaks in that scan. So d·sinθ = mλ is a measurement here, and the readout says how far the peak that was found sits from the order it belongs to.

Formulas
  • Put in — every point of every slit, in phase: A(sinθ) = Σ exp(i·k·ξ·sinθ) over the aperture
  • Which separates, because the slits are alike: A = (Σ one slit) × (Σ slit centres)
  • Sampled — intensity: I = |A|²
  • Searched for — the fringes: local maxima of I down the screen
  • Comes out — the grating equation: d·sinθ = mλ, a·sinθ = mλ for the envelope
  • Approximation — small angle: Δy ≈ λL/d
What to look for
  • Two patterns at once, and in the code they really are two sums. The narrow, evenly spaced fringes are the sum over slit centres and depend only on the separation d. The broad hump they sit inside is the sum across one slit and depends only on its width a. They multiply because the slits are identical — widen a and the envelope narrows, move the slits apart and the fringes crowd together.
  • Orders go missing where the envelope is dark, and the page decides that by measuring rather than by arithmetic. It scans the same aperture with the other slits covered up, and calls an order gone when the envelope there is under a fiftieth of what its neighbours interpolate to. Setting d to a whole multiple of a is the clean way to make that happen, but it is not the only way: at d/a = 3.03 the third order is just as absent, and the whole-number rule does not say so.
  • Add slits and the fringes sharpen: the count of subsidiary maxima between neighbours is N−2, counted off the scan, and the width of a principal maximum falls as 1/N — N times the measured half-height width settles at 0.886 λL/d. That sharpening is also what makes the grating equation true. The envelope leans on each peak and drags it toward the axis, so at two slits the fourth order sits at m = 3.90; the pull dies as 1/N², and by ten slits it is down to three parts in a thousand. A grating is a measuring instrument and a double slit is not.
  • The far field is an assumption. Summing the same aperture with true path lengths instead of k·ξ·sinθ gives a different pattern once the slits span too much of the screen distance, and the readout prints the disagreement: 0.003% of the peak at the defaults, 4% at ten slits, and most of the picture at ten slits 400 µm apart. When that number is large the picture is no longer the far-field pattern anyone means by "diffraction pattern".
