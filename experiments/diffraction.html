Double-Slit Interference & Diffraction

Light through a pair of slits does not make two bright lines. It makes a row of them, evenly spaced, fading in and out under a broader envelope set by the width of a single slit. Both patterns come from the same sum over paths, and where a bright fringe lands on a dark point of the envelope, that order vanishes entirely. Turn on the photon counter and the whole pattern rebuilds itself one dot at a time.

Formulas
  • Far-field intensity: I = I₀(sinα/α)²(sin Nβ/sin β)²
  • α = πa·sinθ/λ, β = πd·sinθ/λ
  • Interference maxima: d·sinθ = mλ
  • Envelope minima: a·sinθ = mλ, m ≠ 0
  • Fringe spacing: Δy ≈ λL/d
What to look for
  • Two patterns at once. The narrow, evenly spaced fringes come from the slits interfering with each other and depend only on the separation d. The broad hump they sit inside comes from each slit's own width a. Widen a single slit and the envelope narrows; move the slits apart and the fringes crowd together.
  • Set d to an exact multiple of a and watch orders disappear. A bright fringe wants to be at d·sinθ = mλ and the envelope is zero at a·sinθ = mλ; when d/a is a whole number p, every p-th order lands exactly on a zero and is simply not there. The readout names the missing orders.
  • Add slits and the bright fringes stay in the same places but get sharper — with N slits there are N−1 zeros between neighbouring maxima, so the peaks narrow as 1/N while the peak height climbs as N². That sharpening is what makes a diffraction grating a measuring instrument.
  • Switch on the photon counter and the pattern arrives one dot at a time, each landing at a random place drawn from the same curve. No single photon has a fringe; the fringes are what a great many of them add up to.
