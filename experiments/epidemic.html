Epidemic — R₀ Counted, Not Assumed

Nobody here is told how contagious the disease is. Individuals meet at random, sometimes pass it on, and recover — and R₀ is counted afterwards out of what actually happened. Then two textbook results the simulation has never heard of turn up on their own: how many end up infected, and how high the wave gets.

Formulas
  • Basic reproduction number: R₀ = β / γ
  • Final size: r = 1 − e^(−R₀·r)
  • Peak infectious: 1 − (1 + ln R₀) / R₀
  • Threshold: R₀ > 1
Notes
  • Nothing in the simulation knows R₀. It is counted: every transmission that happened, divided by the infectious time that was available to cause one, times the average length of an infection. Both halves are tallied as the run goes.
  • The final size is the interesting one. It solves r = 1 − e^(−R₀r), which has no closed form at all — you cannot write the answer down, only find it. The page bisects for it and puts it beside what the population actually did.
  • Contacts are drawn from the whole population, not from a neighbourhood. That is what "well mixed" means, and it is the assumption both formulas rest on — a disease that spreads through a map moves as a wave and ends somewhere else entirely. The moving dots are a picture, not the contact network.
  • Near R₀ = 1 the threshold is a tendency, not a promise. A few seeds can die out by luck in a population that would otherwise burn through, so the verdict reports what this run did rather than what the theory expects.
