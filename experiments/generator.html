Electromagnetic Generator

Water from the faucet turns a wheel, spinning a bar magnet inside a coil of wire. The turning magnet's changing flux induces an AC voltage by Faraday's law — EMF = N·B·A·ω·sin(ωt) — that lights the bulb. Open the faucet, add coils, and watch the voltage rise.

Formulas
  • Flux through coil: Φ = N·B·A·cos(θ)
  • Faraday's law: EMF = −dΦ/dt = N·B·A·ω·sin(ωt)
  • Frequency: f = ω / 2π
  • Wheel: I·dω/dt = τ(flow) − b·ω
Notes
  • Only a changing flux induces a voltage. A magnet held still makes nothing; it's the turning — dΦ/dt — that drives the current, so the faster the wheel spins the brighter the bulb.
  • The output is alternating: as the magnet rotates, the flux swings + to −, so the induced EMF is a sine wave and the voltmeter needle swings both ways.
  • Peak voltage scales with every factor in N·B·A·ω — double the loops, the area, the magnet strength, or the spin rate and you double the peak EMF.
