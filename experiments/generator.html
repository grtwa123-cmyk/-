Electromagnetic Generator

Water from the faucet turns a wheel, spinning a bar magnet inside a coil. Nothing here writes down a sine. The flux the coil links is integrated over its face, cell by cell; the EMF is that flux differentiated in time, with Faraday's minus sign and nothing else. The sine wave, its quarter-turn lag behind the flux, and the way the peak scales are all things the machine produces — and the trace under the picture shows both curves at once.

Formulas
  • Put in — the bore field: B(ρ,θ) = B₀·g(ρ)·(cos θ, sin θ)
  • Summed — flux through a turn: Φ₁ = ∬ B·n̂ dA, on a 32×64 polar grid
  • Differenced — Faraday's law: ε = −(Φ(θ+ωh) − Φ(θ−ωh)) / 2h
  • Comes out: a sine, peak ∝ N·B·ω, lagging Φ by a quarter turn
  • Integrated — the wheel: I·dω/dt = τ(flow) − b·ω
Notes
  • Only a changing flux induces a voltage. Close the faucet and the EMF is exactly zero however strong the magnet is and whatever angle it stopped at — the difference that Faraday's law takes is a difference of nothing.
  • Watch the two curves under the picture. The EMF crosses zero exactly where the flux is at its peak, and peaks where the flux is crossing zero — a quarter turn apart, measured at 90.000°. That offset is not drawn in; it is what differentiating produces.
  • Double the loops, the magnet or the spin and the peak doubles: the measured exponents are 1.000 in all three. Area is the exception. The textbook Φ = N·B·A·cos θ assumes the field is the same everywhere across the coil, and then the peak would be linear in A too. It is not — a wider loop reaches into weaker field, so the measured exponent is about 0.57 and ten times the area buys under four times the EMF.
  • The peak in the readout is not predicted: it is the largest EMF the machine has actually produced within the last complete turn, so it stays blank until the wheel has managed one. Beside the live EMF sits the same law taken off the recorded trace — the logged flux, differentiated across real frames.
