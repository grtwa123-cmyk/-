Formulas
  • Range: R = v₀² · sin(2θ) / g
  • Max height: H = (v₀ · sinθ)² / (2g)
  • Time of flight: T = 2 · v₀ · sinθ / g
  • What is integrated: ẍ = −b|v|vₓ, ẏ = −g − b|v|vᴸ
What to look for
  • Leave the drag at zero and compare the two range readouts. One is measured off the flight — where y actually crosses zero, found by bisection on the last step — and the other is v₀²sin2θ/g. They agree to about one part in 10¹³, which is the integrator proving itself on the one case where the answer is known exactly.
  • Now turn the drag up. The dashed line is the same launch without it, so you can watch the shortfall open up. The arc also stops being symmetric: fired at 45° the ball comes down at 56°, then 67°, because drag takes horizontal speed away and never gives it back.
  • Press Find best angle. Nothing in this page contains the number 45 — the sweep flies 91 launches and reports where the range peaks. In vacuum it comes back 45.00°. With drag the peak slides down the axis: about 39.5° at b = 0.01, about 37° at b = 0.02, and the 45° marker is left behind on the plot.
  • The drag coefficient here is b = CᵈρA/2m, in units of inverse metres, so terminal speed is √(g/b) — about 31 m/s at b = 0.01. Push the launch speed well past that and almost all of it is gone before the apex.
