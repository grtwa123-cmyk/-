Wave Interference

Two coherent point sources radiate circular waves, and nothing beyond their adding together is put in. A screen stands at the right; the intensity is averaged over a whole cycle, scanned down it, and the fringes are located as peaks in that scan. So the fringe spacing beside the picture is a measurement, and it is printed against two predictions: the exact crossing of the hyperbola r₁ − r₂ = qλ, which it sits within a percent of, and the textbook Δy = λL/d, which is 7.6% low here and low everywhere.

Formulas
  • Put in — superposition: ψ = A₁·att₁·cos(k·r₁ − ωt) + A₂·att₂·cos(k·r₂ − ωt + Δφ), att = 1/√r
  • Sampled — intensity: I = ⟨ψ²⟩ over one cycle
  • Searched for — the fringes: local extrema of I down the screen
  • Comes out — the condition: r₁ − r₂ = n·λ bright, (n + ½)·λ dark
  • Compared against — the exact crossing: y(q) = (qλ/2)·√(1 + L² / ((d/2)² − (qλ/2)²))
  • Approximation — narrow and far: Δy ≈ λ·L / d, low by √(1 − (λ/d)²) / √(1 + (d² − λ²)/(4L²))
Notes
  • The bright and dark ticks on the screen are not drawn where a formula says they should be. The intensity is scanned down the screen, its peaks and troughs are found, and each one is refined by fitting a parabola through its three samples — the ticks mark what that search returned, next to the profile it searched.
  • Δy = λL/d is not the law. It is what the law collapses to when the geometry is narrow and the screen is far, and the readout shows the bill: it runs low by exactly √(1 − (λ/d)²) ⁄ √(1 + (d² − λ²)/(4L²)), which is two debts at once. The first is obliquity — λ/d is the sine of the fringe direction while the screen measures its tangent — and no amount of moving the screen back repays it: 0.3% at λ/d = 0.075, 6.1% at 0.343, 18% at 0.571. The second is the near screen, and that one does fade with distance, but here L is only 303 px, so it can be the larger of the two: at λ = 24 px and d = 320 px it swells a 0.3% floor into an 11.8% error.
  • The exact statement is about path difference. Switch the 1/√r spreading off and every located fringe sits where r₁ − r₂ is a whole number of wavelengths to better than one part in a million — and even that is the resolution of the search rather than a property of the field: raise the scan from 1400 samples to 5600 and it falls to a few parts in a hundred million. Leave the spreading on and the envelope tilts each peak slightly toward the axis, so the bright fringes drift a few parts in a thousand, which is the whole of the gap between the measurement and the exact crossing — while the dark ones, pinned by phase rather than amplitude, hold ten to seventy times tighter. Both numbers are in the readout.
  • Turning one source down does not move the fringes, it fades them: the measured visibility follows 2A₁A₂/(A₁²+A₂²) to seven decimal places, reaching 1 only when the two arms are equal. And Δφ slides the whole pattern rigidly — every fringe moves to the crossing for order n + Δφ/2π, so a full turn walks the pattern along by exactly one fringe and leaves it where it started. The spacing at the axis shifts a few percent along the way, which is the screen’s geometry and not the phase: fringes are wider further out.
