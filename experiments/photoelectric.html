Photoelectric Effect

Shine light on a metal plate and it can knock electrons straight out. Turn the brightness all the way up with red light and nothing happens — but a faint violet beam ejects electrons instantly. Light arrives in lumps, and only a lump with enough energy can free an electron.

Formulas
  • Photon energy: E = hf = hc / λ
  • Einstein: KEₔₐₓ = hf − φ
  • Threshold: f₀ = φ / h
  • Stopping voltage: eVₛ = KEₔₐₓ
Notes
  • Below the threshold frequency nothing comes out, no matter how bright the light gets. A wave theory says a dim beam should just take longer to shake an electron loose — it never does.
  • Brightness sets how many electrons come out, never how fast. Only the colour — the energy of one photon — decides the maximum kinetic energy.
  • Push the collector negative and you repel the electrons. The voltage that just stops the fastest of them measures KEₘₐₓ directly, and the slope of that line against frequency is Planck's constant.
